## Implemented acceptance rule

The combined TCP/UDP headers are checked before classification: original packet counts, zero kernel drops, complete two-line headers, and no unparsed packets. The TCP reader reuses the splitter's checks, strictly parses absolute TCP ranges, and compares the checked UDP projection with the analyzer's UDP input. `classify_d120` remains pure; it receives facts and performs no I/O or retries.

A timed-out request passes clause (iv) only if **every** lost transmission overlapping that request or echo range was observed entering the node and never leaving it or arriving at the far collector. The entry must have `position()` equal to `inside` or `boundary` in exactly one verified outer bracket, with the existing 2 µs margin. Both guard upper bound and render→installed must be nonnegative and ≤100 ms. UDP and TCP call the same clause (i)/(ii) helper. Loss before the node, after the node, outside a bracket, on overlapping brackets, or in an unverified/over-bound bracket fails.

The request bytes must later reach the responder; all echo bytes up to that request's end must arrive at the origin in TCP order before `trace_end`. An exact match/stale application token identity confirms that echo. Missing or ambiguous identities fail closed for timeout attribution. No FIN, reset, or second opening connection is allowed through trace end. EOF and every non-timeout application failure still fail. Every unattributed timeout prints its failed conditions in (iv).

TCP sequence intervals are anchored at the captured SYN/SYN-ACK ISNs and the known TEST ONLY `t<sequence>\n` lengths; no payload is read or emitted. Forward/reply transmissions correlate across all four collector positions using direction, IP ID, absolute sequence interval, ACK, flags and TCP timestamp. Ambiguous identity, missing intermediate observations, invalid order, sequence wrap or unparsed syntax is unavailable evidence, never a passing assumption. The fixed lab NAT/endpoint mapping is checked.

The full lab retains traffic from older sockets as well as the measured stream. The opening at the origin before its first send selects the stream. Distinct old ports with no opening in this capture are counted in `ignored_other_connection_packets`; their later FIN/RST cannot close the measured connection. A new origin SYN after the stream starts fails connection continuity, including another port or a reused tuple. The prior gate 2 replay caught this distinction; the initial rejected development replay and the regression proof are retained.

Clause (v) counts **UDP losses + guard-attributed TCP timeouts**, at most five per 100 reloads. Individual recovered segment losses are reported but do not each consume the timeout allowance. The next-UDP-success condition, capture and sidecar integrity checks, generations 1000..1100, and both bounds remain in force.

## TCP stall definition and output

A request's capture stall starts at its first transmission at the origin collector and ends at the first complete, in-order arrival of its echo there. Segmentation, retransmissions and earlier missing echo bytes are included. It excludes application scheduling after arrival and is distinct from send-to-recv monotonic elapsed time. Missing captures produce `null` (unavailable), never zero.

`at10-analysis.json` adds `tcp` facts and summary fields. Its `d120` object and `d120-verdict.txt` add TCP read count and every read record, timeout count, every `tcp_guard_losses` entry (request sequence, absolute byte interval, direction, node-entry time, generation and classification), every guard-attributed timeout with lost transmissions and delivery/echo evidence, `unattributed_timeouts` with failed conditions, combined loss count and `longest_tcp_stall`.
